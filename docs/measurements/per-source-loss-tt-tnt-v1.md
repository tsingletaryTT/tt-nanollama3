## Why this exists

The model's one reported validation loss (**4.2203**) is measured entirely on `wikipedia_simple`: `scripts/blend_corpus.py` writes the nine corpus sources concatenated in sorted-name order, `wikipedia_simple` sorts last, and `train/tokenization.py`'s validation split is the tail 10% of the whole token stream -- which lands entirely inside it. That number describes domain transfer (narrative/naturalist prose -> encyclopedic text), not learning progress on the training mixture as a whole. This table is the fix: loss broken out per source.

## Method, in brief

- **train-sample loss**: sampled from the source's own span of `artifacts/tokens/train_ids.npy` -- **in-distribution training data**, not held out. Reported for every source so the table has a consistent baseline, never confused with the held-out column.
- **held-out loss**: only reported where genuinely unseen text exists for that source (`repetition_factor < 1` in `blend_manifest.json`, meaning the blend used only a prefix of the source's raw file). `wikipedia_simple` uses the existing `val_ids.npy` split; `tinystories` and `poetry` use a sample from the unused tail of their own raw corpus file, confirmed never emitted into the training corpus. The other six sources (`flavour`, `folklore`, `gutenberg_children`, `procedural`, `spine`, `weird`) were each fully consumed by the blend at least once, so no unseen slice of them exists -- their held-out column is `n/a`, not a number standing in for one.
- Both columns report **mean ± standard error over independent windows**, not a bare mean -- see each source's `n=` for how many windows the estimate rests on.

## A second, larger consequence of the same bug: the training mixture itself is skewed

The "share of train" column above is not close to "target share" for most sources, and the
pattern is not noise:

| source | target share | actual share of train_ids | relative change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| wikipedia_simple | 15% | 5.02% | **-66.6%** |
| tinystories | 31% | 35.07% | +13.1% |
| gutenberg_children | 15% | 16.70% | +11.3% |
| spine | 13.5% | 15.04% | +11.4% |
| procedural | 12% | 13.17% | +9.7% |
| folklore | 8% | 8.85% | +10.7% |
| weird | 4% | 4.49% | +12.2% |
| flavour | 0.5% | 0.55% | +9.5% |
| poetry | 1% | 1.12% | +11.8% |

This is not a second bug -- it is the same one, arithmetically. `train/tokenization.py` cuts
the tail 10% of the *whole* token stream off for validation. Every source except
`wikipedia_simple` lies entirely before that cut, so its absolute token count in
`train_ids.npy` is untouched; only the denominator (total train tokens) shrank, by exactly
`wikipedia_simple`'s val-side slice. That shrinks the denominator to ~90% of the full blend,
which is exactly why every other source's *share* of train inflates by close to the same
factor (~1/0.9 = 1.111, i.e. +11%) regardless of that source's own size -- the table above
shows every non-`wikipedia_simple` source landing within about 1-3 points of +11% (+9.5% to
+13.1%), not a random spread. `wikipedia_simple` absorbs the entire opposite effect: it
supplied 100% of what was removed, so its own share collapses instead of inflating.

**Consequence:** the model was not just validated unrepresentatively -- it was *trained* on a
mixture measurably different from the one `train/corpus.py`'s shares specify. Tinystories
(the "backbone" slice, deliberately capped at 31% so the strange slices keep their designed
weight) actually supplied 35% of training tokens; wikipedia_simple (the "grounding" slice)
supplied a third of its intended weight. Every deliberate share decision recorded in
`train/corpus.py`'s rationale comments (see e.g. `spine`'s 13.5%, `flavour`'s 0.5% ceiling)
was honored in the *blend*, then silently un-honored at the tokenization step.

**Does the stratified-split fix (`train/tokenization.py`'s new `source_word_counts` /
`--blend-manifest`) fix this too, or only the validation-representativeness problem?**
It fixes both, and for a structural reason, not as a second patch: holding out the *same*
`val_fraction` from every source's own span (instead of one `val_fraction` cut from the tail
of the whole stream) shrinks every source's train contribution by the identical factor
`(1 - val_fraction)`. A common factor cancels out of every share -- a source's proportion of
`train_ids.npy` under the stratified split is mathematically its proportion of the *whole
corpus*, for every source simultaneously, not just for the one that happens to sort last.
`tests/test_tokenization.py::test_stratified_split_preserves_each_sources_relative_share_of_train`
demonstrates this directly with three sources of deliberately different sizes. This was not
re-derived from these results after the fact -- it falls out of the split design in
`train/tokenization.py::_tokenize_stratified`, and this section exists to make that
consequence explicit rather than leaving it implicit in the arithmetic.

This model (`tt-tnt-v1`) was already trained under the un-stratified split and its
`artifacts/tokens/` arrays are preserved as-is (see the constraints in this repo's
tokenization guard) -- the mixture it actually learned from is the skewed one in the table
above, not the one `train/corpus.py` specifies. The stratified split changes what the *next*
training run sees; it cannot retroactively change what this checkpoint was trained on.

## Reading the results: is the model learning the strange slices, or only the easy ones?

Two things are visible in the table that matter for the next move:

1. **Where train and held-out both exist, they agree.** `tinystories` (1.8214 train-sample vs.
   1.8281 held-out, a 0.0067-nat gap against standard errors of ~0.019 each -- well under 1
   SE) and `poetry` (5.0053 vs. 4.8501, and here held-out is actually *lower*) show no
   train/held-out gap that survives its own noise. At 0.98 epochs on a 22M-parameter model,
   that is the expected signature of **no memorisation** -- and it is the direct,
   independent confirmation that the ~1.2-nat "train/val gap" this investigation started
   from was never a generalisation gap at all. It was entirely the domain mismatch between
   a mostly-narrative training mixture and a wikipedia-only validation set. `wikipedia_simple`
   itself shows the same pattern (4.2014 vs. 4.2847, within 2 SE) -- consistent with a model
   that has generalised about as well on Wikipedia as it ever was going to at this mixture
   share, not one that is overfitting it.

2. **Loss separates cleanly along a narrative/naturalist vs. encyclopedic-and-instructional
   axis, and it is not close.** `tinystories` sits far below every other source (~1.82,
   train and held-out alike) -- expected, since it is deliberately the simplest, most
   repetitive backbone text and the model has seen more of it (proportionally) than the
   corpus recipe even intended (see the mixture-skew section above). `procedural` (3.50) and
   `gutenberg_children` (3.58) come next -- both backbone-adjacent, well-formed prose.
   The narrative/naturalist "strange" slices this project is actually trying to teach a
   voice from -- `spine` (3.84), `folklore` (3.94), `weird` (3.70), `flavour` (3.71) -- sit
   in a fairly tight band roughly 1.7-2.1 nats above tinystories, and `poetry` is highest of
   all (5.01/4.85). `wikipedia_simple`, the encyclopedic grounding slice, sits at 4.20-4.28,
   above every strange slice except poetry.

   **Read honestly, this says the model is learning the strange slices, not ignoring them --
   their loss is well below the ~10.37-nat uniform ceiling and below wikipedia_simple's own
   number, and it is not multiple nats worse than the backbone slices, which is what
   "failing to fit" would look like.** But it is also not caught up to the backbone slices,
   and the gap tracks register (well-formed, repetitive prose vs. archaic/associative/dense
   prose) more than it tracks upsample count or share -- `spine` (2.06x, 13.5% share) and
   `weird` (2.27x, 4% share) land close together despite a 3x difference in share, while
   `flavour` (3.48x upsample, the most-repeated source in the registry at 0.5% share) is
   NOT the best-fit strange slice, sitting mid-pack. That is more consistent with "these
   slices are intrinsically harder to predict" (denser, less repetitive, archaic
   vocabulary) than with "the model hasn't seen enough of them yet."
3. **This is a corpus-and-training-budget question, not obviously a capacity one yet.**
   A 22M-parameter model at 0.98 epochs with a demonstrably skewed mixture (point 2 above)
   has two un-tried levers before "bigger model" is the right conclusion: (a) fix the
   mixture skew (this report's stratified-split fix) and retrain, so the strange slices get
   the share `train/corpus.py` actually specifies instead of ~11% less than that, and
   (b) a longer run / LR decay tail, since one epoch is not long for a corpus this
   deliberately imbalanced toward difficult, low-frequency registers. Neither has been
   tried. A capacity increase would be the right next move only if both of those are tried
   and the strange-slice loss still refuses to close the gap toward the backbone sources'
   train-sample numbers -- that comparison does not exist yet.
